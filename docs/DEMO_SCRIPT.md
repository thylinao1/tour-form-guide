# Demo script (8 to 10 minutes)

This script covers requirements a to e and all 15 demonstration prompts (E1 to E15) in a sensible order. Each step says where to click and gives one sentence to say. Times are a guide.

## Before you start

- Open https://thylinao1.github.io/tour-form-guide/ (or the local `index.html`). It works offline.
- For a clean start use `index.html?welcome=0#race`. The welcome box opens by itself on a first visit, so close it first or use that link.
- Use a laptop screen at full width. The theme button in the header switches light and dark if the room is bright.
- Keep these numbers to hand (they are on the page too).

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Overall winner | Pogacar, 77:02:29 |
| Second, third | Vingegaard +4:24, Lipowitz +11:00 |
| Started, finished all 21 stages | 184 riders, 160 finishers |
| Team classification | Visma 232:01:32, UAE +24:26 |
| Points jersey (official) | Milan 372, Pogacar 294 |
| Mountains jersey (official) | Pogacar 119, Vingegaard 104 |
| Young rider jersey | Lipowitz |
| France | 38 riders and 5 teams |
| Longest stage | Stage 2, 209.1 km |
| Time trials | Stage 5, Caen, 33.0 km, Evenepoel. Stage 13, Peyragudes, 10.9 km, Pogacar |
| Pogacar top ten stage finishes | 13 of 21 |

The yellow total 77:02:29 is longer than the official 76:00:32 for one reason: stage 21 was timed for the overall 50.3 km before the finish, so every total is 3,717 seconds longer. All gaps match the official ones. The page says this in the Method tab.

## The walk

### 1. Start here, about 1 minute (requirement e)

1. Land on **Start here**. Say: "This is a form guide for the 2025 Tour de France. It summarises what already happened, so you can make educated bets. It does not predict anything."
2. Point at the **final podium** and the four counters. Say: "Pogacar won in 77 hours 2 minutes 29 seconds, Vingegaard was 4 minutes 24 seconds behind, Lipowitz third. 184 riders started and 160 reached Paris."
3. Point at **How to make an educated bet**. Say: "Five steps, each one a tab: who is strong overall, what the terrain is, form, the team around the rider, and a head-to-head check."
4. Point at the yellow **Help** button. Say: "A first-time visitor gets a one minute tour, a short video and a glossary here."

### 2. Overall winner, about 1.5 minutes (E1, E7, E10, requirement c)

1. Open **Overall winner**. In **Final overall standings** (E1), click **Vingegaard**. Say: "Total time after 21 stages. Click a rider and the chart on the right shows the day he gained or lost the most time on the final top ten."
2. Read the sentence above the chart. Say: "For Vingegaard it is stage 19, where he was 101.5 seconds faster than the median of the final top ten. Click the bar and it opens that stage." (For Pogacar the biggest day is stage 12, Hautacam, 206.5 seconds faster.)
3. Scroll to **Bonuses and penalties per rider** (E7). Say: "Time bonuses take 10, 6 and 4 seconds off for the first three on a road stage. Pogacar collected 52 seconds in total."
4. In **What if bonuses or penalties did not count?**, untick **Count bonuses**. Say: "After stage 2, switching bonuses off changes the leader from Van der Poel to Pogacar. By the final stage no switch moves anybody, because the gaps are far larger."
5. Scroll to **The fight for yellow, stage by stage** (E10). Use the **Show a team** menu to pick Visma. Say: "Each line is a rider's gap to the leader after every stage. Shaded bands are mountain stages, dashed yellow lines mark every change of leader: stages 2, 5, 6, 7, 10 and 12."
6. Scroll to **Race replay** and press play, then glance at **The race in 3D** and drag it. Say: "The same race as a bar chart race, and as a 3D chart you can rotate."

### 3. Stages, about 1.5 minutes (E2, E11, E15, requirement a)

1. Open **Stages**. In **Stage in focus** (E2), pick **stage 2**. Say: "One stage as a vignette: route, distance, terrain, top finishers with adjusted times. Adjusted time is finish time, minus any bonus, plus any penalty." Then switch to the team view. Say: "This shows which squads filled the top ten that day."
2. In **Roll up and drill down**, open **Mountain**, then open one stage. Say: "Terrain, then stage, then rider. This is the drill down: every total opens into what is inside it." Click any rider name to show the profile drawer, then close it.
3. Scroll to **Against the clock** (E11). Say: "Two time trials. Stage 5 in Caen, 33.0 km, won by Evenepoel. Stage 13 to Peyragudes, 10.9 km, won by Pogacar."
4. Scroll to **The long day** (E15). Say: "Sorted by distance, the longest is stage 2 at 209.1 km, from Lauwin-Planque to Boulogne-Sur-Mer. Its top ten and their adjusted times are in the card." Press **Open this stage** to show it jumps to E2.

### 4. Terrain, about 1 minute (E5, E6)

1. Open **Terrain**. Find **Mountain contours** (E5). Say: "Podium counts and average gap to the winner on the six mountain stages. Click a rider to see the daily results behind the number."
2. Find **Speed by terrain** (E6). Say: "Speed is distance divided by time. A rider whose bar holds up on mountain stages gains when the road climbs. You can switch between riders and teams."

### 5. Jerseys, about 1 minute (E4, requirement b)

1. Open **Jerseys**. In **Official final classifications**, say: "Yellow is Pogacar. Green is Milan with 372 points, Pogacar second with 294. Polka dot is Pogacar with 119, Vingegaard 104. White is Lipowitz."
2. In **Parallel classifications** (E4), say: "Overall place next to young rider place and a mountains-only standing, built from the six mountain stages." Switch to **Age at race start** and move the max age slider. Say: "The official rule is born in 2000 or later. This lets you test another age rule."

### 6. Teams, about 1.5 minutes (E3, E13, E14, requirement c)

1. Open **Teams**. In **Team classification**, say: "Visma won with 232:01:32, UAE second at 24 minutes 26 seconds. The official method adds the three best times of each team on every stage."
2. In **Team burden and contribution** (E3), open a team. Say: "Roll the team down to its riders: who supplied the three counted times, stage by stage."
3. In **Top-ten intensity by team** (E14), click a cell. Say: "Top ten finishes per team and terrain. Visma has the most, 27. Click a cell and you see the stages and riders behind it."
4. Scroll to **Depth of squads** (E13), switch to **Nationalities**, and click UAE. Say: "Every team started with 8. Five teams brought all 8 to Paris. UAE's squad has 8 nationalities. The side card lists every rider with how his race ended."

### 7. Nations, about 1 minute (E8, E9, requirement d)

1. Open **Nations**. In **Where the riders and teams come from** (E8), switch between **Riders** and **Teams**, then click France. Say: "France sent 38 riders and has 5 teams, Belgium 29 riders and 4 teams, the Netherlands 13 riders. Click a country and its riders and teams appear."
2. In **National outcomes** (E9), say: "For each country: best overall place, stage wins, podiums and top tens. Click a row for the riders."

### 8. Form and head-to-head, about 1 minute (E12, betting angle)

1. Open **Form and risk**. In **Consistency near the front** (E12), click the Pogacar bar. Say: "Pogacar finished in the top ten on 13 stages. Click a bar and the stages, places and terrain behind it are listed."
2. Open **Head-to-head**. Say: "Pogacar finished ahead of Vingegaard on 18 of 21 stages, judged on finishing place, not time."

### 9. Bet builder, about 1 minute (betting angle)

1. Open **Bet builder**. Say: "Step 1: what kind of day. Step 2: pick two riders or two teams. Step 3: what 2025 says."
2. Click **Mountain**. Say: "On the six mountain stages Pogacar finished ahead of Vingegaard on 5 of 6. The page marks that as thin evidence because it is only 6 stages."
3. Point at the permanent line. Say: "Settled 2025 results. A frequency from one race, not a forecast. There is no money, stake or bet button anywhere."

### 10. Function map and close, about 1 minute (requirement e, all 15)

1. Open **Function map**. Say: "All 15 prompts, E1 to E15, each with a plain title, the lecturer's title, a live number and a Show me button. 15 of 15 are covered, and the Task 3 list a to e is ticked off below."
2. Press **Start the walk-through** and click **Next** once or twice. Say: "This steps through every prompt and highlights each panel."
3. On any card press **SQL**. Say: "The same question as a short query in DuckDB. The full queries are in `sql/04_functions_e1_e15.sql`."
4. Close with: "Everything is a summary of settled 2025 results. It is not betting advice."

## Questions the grader may ask

**Where do the points and mountains numbers come from?**
The race data has no intermediate sprint or climb points, so those two jerseys use published standings. The top 10 of each is the official result (letour.fr and other sources, cross-checked). The full lists (132 and 55 riders) were collected from CyclingDB and their top 10s match the official ones. The details are in `data/official/official_sources.md`.

**Why is the winner's total 77:02:29 when the official total is 76:00:32?**
Because of rain, stage 21 was timed for the overall 50.3 km before the finish. Our data holds the full stage time for everyone, so every total is longer by the same 3,717 seconds. All gaps are identical to the official ones.

**Why are 160 riders ranked and not 184?**
Only riders with a result on every stage counted are ranked. Without that rule, riders who left early would have the smallest totals and appear at the top. 24 riders left the race: 12 did not start a stage and 12 withdrew. Two (bibs 64 and 154) left on stage 1 and have no results.

**How is Best Team (team classification) computed?**
On each stage, add the three best individual finish times of the team, then add the 21 stage totals. Bonuses and penalties are not included. This reproduces the official top 10 exactly: Visma 232:01:32, UAE +24:26.

**Which young rider rule is used?**
Born on or after 1 January 2000 (2025 regulations, article 24d). Using it on our data reproduces the official top 10 in order and to the second. The age slider in E4 lets you try another rule, but the default is the official one.

**How is the pivotal stage defined?**
For a rider, it is the stage with the largest difference between his adjusted time and the median adjusted time of the final top ten riders on that stage. Vingegaard: stage 19 (101.5 seconds faster). Pogacar: stage 12 (206.5 seconds faster).

**Why does removing bonuses change nothing at the finish?**
The bonuses are small (10, 6 and 4 seconds) next to the final gaps (Vingegaard 264 seconds behind, Lipowitz 660). They did change the lead early: after stage 2 the leader is Van der Poel with bonuses and Pogacar without them. The top ten places at the finish do not move.

**Why a custom web dashboard instead of Tableau or Power BI?**
The brief allows a web-based platform. A single offline file is easy to share and demo, and it let us build drill down, side by side comparison, replay and 3D charts exactly as we wanted.

**What OLAP operations does it support?**
Roll up (totals by terrain, team, country), drill down (terrain to stage to rider, team to rider to stage, country to riders), slice (one stage, one terrain, one week), dice (several filters at once, such as a team and a stage type), and pivot (team by terrain grid, jerseys side by side). The Function map tab shows which operation each prompt uses.

**Where is the SQL?**
In `sql/`. `01_normalized_schema.sql` is the Task 2 schema, `03_star_schema.sql` is the star schema, `04_functions_e1_e15.sql` has one query per prompt, and `05_task4_compare.sql` compares both schemas. `tests/sql_check.sh` runs them in DuckDB and checks 21 results.

**Why a betting angle, and why is it not a forecast?**
The brief asks for a useful, easy to read dashboard. A form guide is a familiar way to present performance: who is strong, on which terrain, how reliable. We only use settled 2025 results, from a single race, so the numbers describe the past. The Bet builder shows a record, a smoothed share and how thin the evidence is. It never shows a stake, a payout or a place bet button.
